MODEL & ASSUMPTIONS

A dynamic fracture experiment

Why a second model. The XFEM page grows one crack at a time from an energy criterion, which is the right lesson for stable crack growth. A hammer blow on glass is a different physical event: stress waves race outward, running cracks become unstable and branch, and small variations in the material decide where. Bond-based peridynamics captures that with almost no extra machinery.

What is computed. The plate is a cloud of nodes, each bonded to every neighbour within a horizon of three spacings. A bond carries a force proportional to its stretch and breaks permanently once the stretch exceeds the critical value s₀ = √(4πGc/(9Eδ)) that follows from the fracture energy. Newton’s second law is integrated explicitly in time. Broken bonds are the cracks; there is no crack tracking and no branching rule.

The hammer. Nodes under the hammer head are pushed outward as the dent forms, over a short ramp, with a push that grows with the impact energy m·g·h. This in-plane push stands in for the bending and contact stresses of a face-on blow and reproduces the radial cracks and their branches; it does not reproduce the concentric ring cracks of a true face-on impact.

Material structure. Node strengths are scattered randomly (lognormal), as in real glass. That scatter is what makes the cracks wander and fork; the nearly uniform option shows how tidy and symmetric the same blow becomes without it.

Current limits. Two-dimensional, plane stress, Poisson’s ratio fixed at 1/3 by the bond-based formulation, no fragments flying off (broken pieces stay in place), a no-fail zone near the free edges to suppress spurious edge damage from reflected waves, and node positions are jittered to keep cracks from following the grid. It is a teaching model, not a validated shatter simulation.
