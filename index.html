MODEL & ASSUMPTIONS

A small XFEM experiment

The Python solver computes a scalar out-of-plane displacement field on a fixed triangular mesh. Crack faces use shifted Heaviside enrichment; tips use the Mode III square-root enrichment. The left edge is held fixed.

The hammer is represented by a pair of opposing out-of-plane shear loads at the right edge. Its drop energy is converted to a peak-equivalent load using an assumed 0.5 mm stopping distance. This is a quasi-static teaching approximation, not a resolved hammer/contact simulation.

A virtual crack extension estimates energy release. Growth is allowed when the released energy per new crack area exceeds the selected fracture energy. Each strike advances at most one increment; strike again to continue, or aim elsewhere to nucleate another crack.

Cracks follow straight horizontal paths. Nucleation uses a finite trial notch, so it is mesh dependent. There is no plasticity, fatigue, branching, crack contact, or dynamic fragmentation. The small remaining ligament is marked as separated once it is within one growth increment. Visual crack width is exaggerated.

Materials are illustrative elastic presets. Fine meshes cost more time and do not make this an experimentally validated prediction.
